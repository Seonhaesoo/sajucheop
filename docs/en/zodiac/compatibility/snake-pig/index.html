The two signs

Snake — the deep strategistSnakes say little and notice everything. Their judgment is usually right, and they seldom show their whole hand.
Pig — the generous optimistPigs are frank, kind-hearted and trusting. They give freely, and people gather around them because of it.

How the branches meet — Opposites

The Snake (巳) and the Pig (亥) sit directly opposite each other on the zodiac wheel. There can be a strong pull at first, but your methods run in opposite directions, so collisions are frequent: when one moves, the other stops; when one talks, the other shuts down. This is less a bad bond than a different one. Stop trying to change each other, turn the difference into a division of labor, and you can do together what neither could alone.

The elements

The Pig’s Water controls the Snake’s Fire, as water tames fire. The Pig tends to lead while the Snake adapts; balance comes when the Pig eases off and the Snake speaks up. The tension also tempers you both.

In love and marriage

Strong chemistry, big arguments over small things. If you marry, agree on the house rules early. Snakes warm up slowly and then love with great intensity. Once they trust you, they protect the bond fiercely. Pigs love sincerely and accept partners as they are. They are happiest with someone as straightforward as they are.

As friends and colleagues

Better at a little distance. You work best in different roles rather than side by side. Snakes do their best work in analysis, strategy, research, finance and specialist fields, where long preparation pays off. Pigs do well with people and in teams — hospitality, healthcare, charity and sales — and quietly build wealth through consistency.

As family

Between parent and child, friction tends to peak in the teenage years; space works better than control. The Snake’s weak spot is suspicion; the Pig’s is difficulty saying no. Knowing that solves half the problem.

Making it work

  • Never settle an argument on the day it starts
  • Put rules about money, time and chores in writing
  • Stop trying to fix the other person’s way of doing things
