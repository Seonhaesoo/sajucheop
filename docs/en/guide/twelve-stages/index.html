Twelve Life Stages — 十二運星

The Twelve Life Stages (sibi unseong, 십이운성, 十二運星) measure how much strength a heavenly stem finds on each of the twelve earthly branches, and they draw that measurement as a human life. A stem is born on one branch, is bathed, comes of age, takes office, reaches its peak, then declines, sickens, dies, is laid in the grave, is cut off, is conceived again and is nurtured in the womb until the next Birth. The same Jia Wood that is a fresh sprout on the Pig is a tree in full leaf on the Rabbit and a tree gone quiet in the heat of the Horse. Twelve pictures, one stem.

In practice the stages are read from the Day Master, the stem of your day pillar. The branch beneath it, the day branch, gives the stage that readers weigh most heavily: it is treated as your basic constitution and as the seat of your spouse. The month branch shows the strength you bring to work and public life, the year branch your early years and roots, the hour branch the later years and your children. The same table is then laid over time. Every ten-year luck pillar and every year has a branch, so each period of your life sits on one of the twelve stages as well.

How to read the stages

Find your Day Master's row in the table and read across. Yang stems (Jia, Bing, Wu, Geng, Ren) move forward through the branches from their Birth branch; yin stems (Yi, Ding, Ji, Xin, Gui) move backward. Wu and Ji share the Fire stems' positions, the traditional method used throughout Sajucheop. Then take the four branches of your chart and note the stage under each, starting with the day branch. A stage describes how energy behaves, not how good your luck is: Death and Grave are working states, and Prosperity can be too much of a good thing.

The full table

Rows are the ten Day Masters, columns the twelve branches from Rat to Pig; each cell names the stage that stem reaches on that branch. Read along your own row. Yang rows run forward from Birth, yin rows backward, and the Wu and Ji rows repeat Bing and Ding. Every stage appears exactly once in each row.

The twelve, in order

  • Birth 長生 — A newborn taking its first breath: small, fresh, and helped from every side.
  • Bath 沐浴 — The newborn is washed: bare, sensitive, charming, and chilled by the first draft.
  • Cap and Belt 冠帶 — A youth at the coming-of-age rite, cap on and belt tied, eager to be treated as an adult.
  • Official 建祿 (臨官) — A grown man takes office and draws his own salary: standing on his own feet at last.
  • Prosperity 帝旺 — An emperor at the height of his reign: nothing above him, and the descent already in view.
  • Decline 衰 — Past the summit, a seasoned elder who no longer rushes and no longer needs to.
  • Sickness 病 — An elder grown frail and tender: less strength for the world, more feeling for it.
  • Death 死 — Movement stops and the eyes turn inward: the stillness of a life that has been lived.
  • Grave 墓 — Laid to rest in the family storehouse: gathered in, kept safe, waiting to be opened.
  • Extinction 絕 — The thread is cut: nothing remains of the old form, and nothing yet of the new.
  • Conception 胎 — A life just conceived: real, invisible, and shaped entirely by what surrounds it.
  • Nurture 養 — Growing in the womb: fed, sheltered, unhurried, and nearly ready to be born.

FAQ

What are the Twelve Life Stages?

A cycle that describes how strong a heavenly stem is on each of the twelve branches, drawn as a life: Birth, Bath, Cap and Belt, Official, Prosperity, Decline, Sickness, Death, Grave, Extinction, Conception and Nurture. Readers use it for the day pillar, the month branch and luck pillars.

Which stage is my day pillar?

Take your Day Master (the day stem) and your day branch and read the table: the cell where they meet is the stage your day pillar sits on. The calculator shows your day pillar, and every sixty-pillar page names its stage.

Are Death, Sickness and Grave bad?

No. They describe how the Day Master’s energy behaves on that branch — inward, quiet, storing — not events. Many focused, scholarly and patient charts sit on exactly those stages.
